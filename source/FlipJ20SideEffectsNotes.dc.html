Side Effects at a Glance

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Most reported side effects are minor and short-lived; a temporary pain flare and access-site bruising or tenderness are common and usually settle within days to about two weeks.
  • Less likely effects include patchy skin discoloration over the knee, a small skin sore that usually heals on its own, foot tingling or numbness, and access-site hematoma, usually temporary.
  • Rare risks include contrast reaction, contrast-related kidney strain, artery injury, infection, blood clot and nontarget embolization injuring skin, muscle or nerve.

Treatment options

  • Screening for PAD, kidney function, allergy and bleeding risk
  • Careful mapping and slow, controlled particle delivery
  • Clear aftercare instructions and scheduled follow-up

Ask your patient

  • Do you have kidney disease, diabetes or a dye allergy?
  • Do you take blood thinners or have bleeding problems?
  • Who can drive you home and check on you that day?

Refer when

  • Groin swelling, bleeding or pain that is getting worse.
  • Skin breakdown, a sore, or spreading color change over the knee.
  • Fever, calf swelling, or a cold, numb or weak foot.
