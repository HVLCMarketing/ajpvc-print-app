Embolization Beyond the Knee

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Frozen shoulder, tennis elbow, plantar fasciitis, hip and hand OA: evidence is mostly small case series without controls.
  • After knee replacement, embolization has an established role for recurrent bleeding; data for persistent pain are early.
  • Consider only after a confirmed diagnosis and several months of standard care; results are uncertain.

Treatment options

  • Frozen shoulder: physical therapy, injections, time
  • Tennis elbow: activity modification, physical therapy, time
  • Plantar fasciitis: stretching, footwear, orthotics, physical therapy
  • Embolization for selected patients not improving with standard care

Ask your patient

  • How long has the pain lasted despite treatment?
  • Has imaging ruled out a tear, fracture or nerve compression?
  • Do you understand the evidence is still early?

Refer when

  • Sudden pop with weakness pushing off: prompt assessment for Achilles rupture.
  • Pain after knee replacement: exclude infection, loosening and instability first.
  • Recurrent bleeding into a replaced knee: embolization evaluation.
