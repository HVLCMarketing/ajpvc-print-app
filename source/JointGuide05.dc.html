Joint & tendon pain guide

Evaluating Your Joint Pain

Before any treatment, your specialist will learn about your pain and look for its cause. This helps make sure a procedure is right for you.

Your Medical History

Your specialist will ask about:

  • Where it hurts, and for how long
  • What makes the pain better or worse
  • Treatments you’ve tried, such as medicine, therapy, injections or surgery
  • Other health problems, such as diabetes, kidney disease or poor circulation
  • Medicines you take, especially blood thinners
  • Allergies, especially to X-ray contrast dye or iodine

Your Physical Exam

Your specialist checks the painful area for swelling, tenderness and how well it moves, and may check the pulses in your arms or legs.

Imaging Tests

X-rays show how much cartilage has worn away. An MRI or ultrasound may be used to look at tendons and the joint lining. Bring any recent imaging to your visit.

Rating Your Pain

You may be asked to rate your pain from 0 to 10 and answer a few questions about daily activities. Doing this again after treatment helps you and your doctor track your progress.
