Understanding Joint & Tendon Pain

Does pain in your knee, hip, shoulder, hand or heel keep you from doing the things you love? You’re not alone. Joint and tendon pain is one of the most common reasons adults see a doctor. This booklet explains what causes it, how it’s evaluated and the treatment options available, including minimally invasive embolization procedures.

What Is Osteoarthritis?

Osteoarthritis is the most common type of arthritis. Cartilage, the smooth tissue that cushions the ends of your bones, slowly wears down. The lining of the joint can become inflamed and swollen. The result is pain, stiffness and trouble moving.

What Is Tendon Pain?

Tendons connect muscles to bones. Fascia is a tough band of tissue, like the one along the bottom of your foot. When these tissues are strained over and over, they can stay painful for months. Examples include frozen shoulder, rotator cuff tendon problems and plantar fasciitis (heel pain).

Who Gets Joint Pain?

  • Adults over 50
  • People who carry extra weight
  • People with a past joint injury or surgery
  • Those whose work or sports stress a joint
  • People with a family history of arthritis

What Can Be Done?

Many people get relief from self-care, physical therapy, medicine or injections. When pain continues despite these, your doctor may talk with you about other options. Use this booklet as a starting point for that conversation.
