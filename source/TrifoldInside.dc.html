A less invasive option for joint pain

Osteoarthritis is a progressive disease. Over time, chronic inflammation causes pain and can speed the breakdown of cartilage inside the joint.

For mild to moderate pain, doctors often recommend medication, physical therapy, or gel or steroid injections. Joint replacement is the standard treatment for severe arthritis.

But not everyone is able, or ready, to have surgery, and a lifetime of pain medication isn't right for everyone either.

Today there's another option to ask about: joint embolization.

How embolization works

Embolization is a minimally invasive, outpatient procedure. A specialist places a thin catheter into an artery in your upper thigh or wrist, then uses X-ray imaging to guide it to the small arteries feeding the inflamed lining of the joint.

Tiny particles are released to reduce blood flow to the inflamed tissue. The goal is to calm inflammation and ease pain.

What causes osteoarthritis?

Cartilage cushions the ends of your bones so joints move smoothly. As it wears down, bones rub together, causing inflammation, pain, stiffness and swelling.

Common risk factors include:

  • Age and family history
  • Carrying extra weight
  • An old joint injury
  • Work or sports that stress the joint
