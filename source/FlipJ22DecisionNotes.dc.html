A Step-by-Step Decision Path

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Persistent knee pain starts with defining goals and confirming the diagnosis by history, exam and X-ray, with MRI when needed; other sources are treated directly.
  • For knee OA, education, exercise, PT, weight management and medications come first, with reassessment after an adequate trial; if relief is enough, continue and monitor.
  • If not, options include injections, GAE, nerve ablation and an orthopedic opinion; mild-to-moderate, pain-driven OA may suit injections or GAE, while advanced OA with deformity warrants orthopedic evaluation.

Treatment options

  • Injections or GAE for mild–moderate, pain-driven OA
  • Nerve ablation in selected patients
  • Orthopedic evaluation for advanced OA or deformity

Ask your patient

  • What would you do if your knee hurt less?
  • What have you tried, for how long, and how did it work?
  • How will we measure success: pain, distance or an activity?

Refer when

  • Hip, spine, tendon or nerve source: treat the true source.
  • Inflammatory arthritis features: rheumatology referral.
  • Advanced OA with deformity: orthopedic surgical evaluation.
