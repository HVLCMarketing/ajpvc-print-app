Clinical focus · GAE and TAME

Match the intervention to the diagnosis

Persistent symptoms, functional loss, prior treatment, and imaging should guide the evaluation.

How GAE is designed to work: an inflamed lining with abnormal new vessels; angiography finds the “blush”; tiny particles slow flow in selected branches; inflammation and pain may settle.

GAE for symptomatic knee osteoarthritis

Genicular artery embolization selectively targets abnormal periarticular vascularity associated with synovial inflammation. Its purpose is symptom and functional improvement; it does not restore cartilage or correct mechanical deformity.

Consider referral for patients with clinically and radiographically supported knee osteoarthritis whose pain and activity limitations persist despite appropriate conservative management. Evaluation should address the pain generator, arthritis severity, vascular anatomy, comorbidities, and whether orthopedic management remains the more appropriate pathway.

The Society of Interventional Radiology’s 2026 position statement supports GAE for selected patients after unsuccessful conservative therapy who are not candidates for, or wish to delay, total knee arthroplasty, and calls for larger randomized trials. This supports a defined clinical role, not universal candidacy or equivalence to joint replacement.

TAME for selected hip, shoulder, and hand conditions

Transcatheter arterial microembolization targets abnormal small-vessel supply associated with selected chronic inflammatory musculoskeletal conditions. The painful body region alone does not establish candidacy. Referral should identify the suspected diagnosis and the resulting functional limitation.

AreaUseful referral detail
HipPain distribution, walking and sleep limitations, imaging, prior rehabilitation or injections, and evaluation of competing spinal or structural causes.
ShoulderRange-of-motion loss, suspected adhesive capsulitis or other diagnosis, imaging, and response to rehabilitation, medication, or injections.
HandSpecific symptomatic joints, osteoarthritis or other suspected diagnosis, grip and dexterity limitations, and prior hand therapy or medication.

Evidence remains indication-specific. Published reports include a 2025 retrospective shoulder study in refractory adhesive capsulitis and a 2026 retrospective hip osteoarthritis study; hand evidence includes small early studies. Knee results should not be generalized to every hip, shoulder, or hand condition. Counseling should address expected benefit, uncertainty, alternatives, access-site and contrast risks, and nontarget embolization. Do not delay indicated orthopedic, rheumatologic, or other specialist care.

Document function, not only pain intensity

Walking distance · stair tolerance · sleep · dressing · grip · work demands

Define an activity goal that can be revisited after treatment.
