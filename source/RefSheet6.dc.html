Dialysis Access, Carotid Disease & HBOT

Keeping dialysis access working, preventing stroke, and healing wounds that have stalled.

Refer when

✓Dialysis access: hard cannulation, prolonged bleeding after needle removal, arm or face swelling, a change in thrill, falling adequacy or rising venous pressures, aneurysm
✓Access, call same day: no thrill or bruit (clotted access)
✓Carotid, urgent: evaluated TIA, amaurosis fugax or minor stroke with 50 to 99% stenosis
✓Carotid: asymptomatic stenosis of 70% or more (60 to 69%, case by case)
✓HBOT: diabetic lower-extremity wound, Wagner grade III or higher, not healing after 30 days of standard care
✓HBOT: delayed radiation injury, or compromised grafts and flaps

What we offer

Dialysis accessFistulagram, angioplasty and stenting
CarotidCarotid duplex and angiography; stroke-prevention planning
HBOTHyperbaric oxygen therapy alongside wound care
TestingRenal artery and abdominal aortic ultrasound

Send with the referral

•Dialysis unit notes and recent access flow data
•Carotid imaging reports and symptom dates
•Wound history, Wagner grade and 30-day care record

Evidence at a glance

KDOQIIntervene on clinical signs of access dysfunction, not surveillance findings alone.1
≤14 daysRevascularization is favored within 14 days of symptoms for symptomatic carotid stenosis.2
Grade DUSPSTF recommends against screening asymptomatic adults for carotid stenosis; image when symptoms or findings warrant.3
30 daysMedicare may cover HBOT for Wagner III+ diabetic wounds if 30 days of standard care show no measurable healing.4
EmergenciesStroke symptoms: call 911. A new TIA not yet evaluated belongs in the ED. Access bleeding that won’t stop, or an aneurysm with thin or broken skin: ED.
