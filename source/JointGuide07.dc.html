How Embolization Works

Embolization is done by an interventional specialist, a doctor trained to treat problems inside blood vessels using small tubes and X-ray guidance.

The catheter reaches a small artery feeding the inflamed tissue.

Tiny particles flow into the extra vessels.

Blood flow to the inflamed tissue is reduced.

1

A small puncture

Your specialist numbs the skin and places a thin, flexible tube called a catheter into an artery, usually in the wrist or the top of the thigh.

2

Guided by X-ray

Using live X-ray and contrast dye, the catheter is steered to the small arteries that feed the inflamed area.

3

Tiny particles

Microscopic particles are released to reduce blood flow in the extra vessels. Other arteries keep supplying blood to the bone and surrounding tissue.

4

All done

The catheter is removed, and pressure or a small closure device seals the puncture. No stitches are needed.
