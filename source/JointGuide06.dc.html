Joint & tendon pain guide

Your Treatment Plan

After your evaluation, you and your specialist will make a plan together. Treatment usually starts with the simplest options.

Treatment Options

1Self-careActivity changes, weight management, heat or ice
2Physical therapyExercises to build strength and flexibility
3MedicinePain relievers and anti-inflammatory medicines
4InjectionsSteroid or gel injections into the joint
5EmbolizationMinimally invasive, outpatient procedure
6SurgerySuch as joint replacement, for severe arthritis

Is Embolization Right for Me?

You may be a candidate if you:

  • Have had joint or tendon pain for 3 months or more
  • Haven’t found enough relief with medicine, therapy or injections
  • Have arthritis or inflammation seen on imaging
  • Aren’t ready for, can’t have, or want to delay surgery

It May Not Be Right If You Have

  • An active infection
  • Severe arthritis better treated with joint replacement
  • A severe allergy to contrast dye that can’t be managed
  • Advanced kidney disease or a bleeding disorder
  • Serious blockages in the arteries of the arm or leg
  • Pregnancy

Questions to Ask Your Doctor

  • Am I a good candidate, and why?
  • What results can I realistically expect?
  • What are the risks for someone like me?
  • What other treatments could I try first?
  • Will my insurance cover it, and what will I pay?
