After GAE: What’s Normal and When to Call

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Skin discoloration happens when embolic material reaches tiny skin vessels; it usually fades over days to weeks.
  • Small skin ulcers occasionally occur and usually heal on their own with monitoring.
  • At the access site, a small bruise is common; a firm, growing or very painful swelling is not.

Treatment options

  • Pain relief as recommended by the care team
  • Monitor and treat any skin breakdown
  • Prompt evaluation of access-site swelling or bleeding

Ask your patient

  • Any fever, chills, or spreading redness or warmth?
  • Is your foot cold, pale, blue or numb?
  • Is the skin over the knee blistering or darkening?

Refer when

  • Cold, numb leg or heavy bleeding: call 911.
  • Severe or rapidly worsening knee pain not controlled by recommended relief.
  • Numbness or tingling in the foot or leg that does not go away.
