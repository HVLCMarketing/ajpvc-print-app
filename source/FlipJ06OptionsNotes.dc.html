Your Treatment Options, From Self-Care to Surgery

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Education, exercise, physical therapy and weight management are core OA care, not just a waiting period.
  • Steroid injections give short-term relief for some; evidence for hyaluronic acid and PRP is mixed.
  • GAE and replacement are not interchangeable; replacement suits advanced structural disease when nonsurgical care has failed.

Treatment options

  • Exercise, PT, weight management, bracing, topical or oral NSAIDs
  • Steroid injection for short-term relief; HA and PRP evidence mixed
  • GAE for persistent knee OA pain in selected patients
  • Osteotomy, partial or total knee replacement when indicated

Ask your patient

  • What would you do if your knee hurt less?
  • Which treatments have you tried, for how long, and did they help?
  • Are you hoping to avoid or delay surgery?

Refer when

  • Advanced OA with substantial pain and functional loss despite nonsurgical care: orthopedic assessment.
  • Persistent symptomatic knee OA despite appropriate care: GAE consultation.
  • Features of inflammatory arthritis: rheumatology.
