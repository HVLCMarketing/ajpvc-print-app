Sclerotherapy & Phlebectomy

Smaller veins and bulging surface veins can be treated with injections or removed through tiny nicks in the skin, often along with vein closure.

Ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy

A medicine is injected into the vein, causing it to close. Ultrasound guides the needle to veins below the skin’s surface.

Spider vein sclerotherapy

Tiny injections fade small surface veins. Several sessions may be needed. Often considered cosmetic.

Ambulatory phlebectomy

After numbing, bulging veins are removed through tiny nicks. Stitches usually aren’t needed, and you walk out afterward.

What to Expect After a Vein Procedure

Right away

Walk for a few minutes before you leave. You can usually go home within an hour or two.

First few days

Walk often and wear compression as directed. Bruising, tenderness or a tight, pulling feeling along the treated vein is common.

Within about a week

You may have a follow-up ultrasound to check the result and make sure there is no clot.

Weeks to months

Treated veins fade gradually. Symptoms such as aching and heaviness often improve as healing continues.

Tips for Recovery

  • Avoid heavy lifting and hard exercise for a few days, or as advised
  • Ask before long flights or car trips in the first weeks
  • Avoid hot tubs and baths until your specialist says it’s okay
