What the Angiogram Shows

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Contrast angiography can show abnormal vascular staining, often called a blush, that may correspond to inflamed tissue and the patient’s painful region.
  • The blush is interpreted alongside pain location, anatomy and clinical findings; not every visible vessel is a treatment target.
  • A typical endpoint is reduced abnormal staining with flow preserved in the parent artery; the figure is a simplified illustration, not a patient image.

Treatment options

  • Mark the most painful spot before the procedure
  • Selective angiography of each candidate branch
  • Completion imaging to check the endpoint

Ask your patient

  • Can you point to the spot where it hurts most?
  • Have you ever reacted to X-ray dye?
  • Do you have kidney problems or diabetes?

Refer when

  • Prior contrast reaction: individualized plan before angiography.
  • Reduced kidney function: check labs and minimize contrast.
  • Blush that does not match the painful area: reassess the pain source.
