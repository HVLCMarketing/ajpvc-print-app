Peripheral Artery Disease

Part 1 · Arteries

Peripheral artery disease (PAD) happens when fatty plaque builds up inside the arteries of the legs, narrowing them and reducing blood flow.

Common Symptoms

  • Cramping or pain in the calf, thigh or hip when walking that eases with rest
  • Foot pain at rest, often at night
  • Cold, numb or pale feet
  • Sores on the toes, feet or legs that heal slowly
  • Shiny skin, hair loss or weak pulses in the legs

Many people with PAD have no symptoms at first.

Why It Matters

PAD can be a sign that arteries elsewhere, in the heart and brain, are also affected, raising the risk of heart attack and stroke. Severe PAD can threaten the leg itself.

Risk Factors

  • Smoking
  • Diabetes
  • High blood pressure or cholesterol
  • Age over 50
  • Kidney disease
