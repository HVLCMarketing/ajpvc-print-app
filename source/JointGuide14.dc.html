Joint & tendon pain guide

Risks & When to Call

Embolization is minimally invasive, but like any procedure it has risks. Your specialist will talk with you about them before you decide.

Possible Risks and Complications

  • Bruising or bleeding at the puncture site
  • Temporary skin color change or tenderness over the treated area, which usually fades in days to weeks
  • A short-term increase in joint pain
  • Allergic reaction to contrast dye
  • Infection
  • Injury to the artery
  • Strain on the kidneys from contrast dye, mainly in people with kidney disease
  • Rarely, particles reaching nearby tissue, which can cause skin or tissue damage
!

When to Call Your Doctor

Call 661-324-4100 right away if you have:

  • Bleeding or swelling at the puncture site that doesn’t stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
  • Fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher, or chills
  • Increasing redness, warmth, pain or drainage at the puncture site
  • Skin over the treated area that turns dark, blisters or opens
  • Numbness, tingling, coldness or color change in your arm, hand, leg or foot
  • Joint pain that gets much worse instead of better

Call 911 if you have

Heavy bleeding that won’t stop with firm pressure, chest pain, trouble breathing or signs of a stroke.

For urgent concerns after hours, call 661-324-4100.
