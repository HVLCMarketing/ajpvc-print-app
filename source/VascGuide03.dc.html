How Leg Veins Work

Your leg veins have a tough job: moving blood uphill, back to your heart.

Healthy Valves

Inside your veins are small one-way valves. When your calf muscles squeeze, blood moves up. The valves close so it can’t fall back down.

When Valves Fail

If the valves weaken, blood leaks backward and pools in the lower legs. This is called venous reflux, or chronic venous insufficiency. Pressure builds, veins stretch and bulge, and fluid can leak into the skin.

Surface (superficial) veins

Lie just under the skin. Varicose veins usually start here, often in the great or small saphenous vein.

Deep veins

Run through the muscles and carry most of the blood back to the heart. A blood clot here (DVT) is more serious.

Why This Matters

Treating the leaking vein lowers the pressure in your legs. That can ease aching, heaviness and swelling, and help protect your skin.
