Genicular Artery Embolization (GAE)

GAE treats knee pain caused by osteoarthritis. It targets the genicular arteries, the small blood vessels that supply the lining of the knee.

Who May Benefit

  • Mild to moderate knee osteoarthritis with ongoing pain
  • Pain that hasn’t improved after 3 months of medicine, therapy or injections
  • People not ready for, or not able to have, knee replacement
  • Some people with ongoing pain after knee replacement

How It Works

The catheter is usually placed in an artery at the top of the thigh. Contrast dye maps the genicular arteries and shows areas of extra blood flow, which often match where your knee hurts. Particles are then released into those small branches.

What the Research Shows

GAE is a newer procedure. Most studies so far have been small, and many had no comparison group. Studies comparing GAE with a placebo (sham) procedure have had mixed results, and larger trials are under way. Results vary from person to person.
