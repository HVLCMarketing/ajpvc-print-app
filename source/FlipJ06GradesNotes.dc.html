How Knee Arthritis Is Graded

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Kellgren–Lawrence grades radiographic OA 0 to 4: none, doubtful, definite but mild, moderate, severe.
  • The grade reflects osteophytes, joint-space narrowing, sclerosis and deformity — it is not a measure of pain.
  • Some patients have major structural change with little pain; others have significant pain with milder X-ray findings.

Treatment options

  • Knee X-ray as the first imaging test
  • MRI when X-rays are nondiagnostic or specific problems suspected
  • Plan treatment on symptoms, function and imaging together
  • GAE typically considered for mild-to-moderate OA; some advanced cases too

Ask your patient

  • Does your X-ray match how your knee feels day to day?
  • What activities has your knee pain stopped you from doing?

Refer when

  • Grade 4 with major deformity: orthopedic assessment — do not delay it solely to pursue GAE.
  • Severe pain with only mild X-ray changes: look for other pain sources (hip, spine, meniscus).
