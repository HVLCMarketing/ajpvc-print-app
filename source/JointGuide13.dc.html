Joint & tendon pain guide

After Your Procedure

Most people go home a few hours after the procedure. Recovery is usually quick, but relief often builds gradually.

What to Expect

Day of procedure

Rest at home. Keep the puncture site clean and dry. An adult should drive you home and stay with you overnight.

First few days

Some soreness in the joint and a small bruise at the puncture site are normal. Short walks are fine. Avoid heavy lifting and hard exercise.

Weeks 1 to 4

Pain relief often starts gradually. Return to your usual activities as your specialist advises.

Months 1 to 3

Follow-up visits track your progress. The full effect may take several weeks to a few months.

Caring for the Puncture Site

  • Keep the bandage on and the site dry for 24 hours, or as instructed
  • You can shower after 24 hours. Avoid baths, hot tubs and swimming until the site heals
  • A small bruise or a pea-sized lump is normal
  • Don’t lift anything heavy with the arm or leg used for the puncture for a few days

Managing Discomfort

  • Some soreness in the treated joint for a few days is normal
  • Use the pain reliever your specialist recommends
  • Ice the joint for 15 to 20 minutes at a time if advised

Keep Moving

As pain eases, gentle exercise or physical therapy helps build strength and keep the joint moving. Ask your specialist when to start.
