Arterial & venous guide

Angiogram, Angioplasty & Stenting

These procedures find and open narrowed arteries from the inside, through a small puncture. No large incision is needed.

Angiogram

Contrast dye and X-ray create a map of your arteries. The catheter usually goes in through a small puncture in the groin or wrist, sometimes the foot.

Angioplasty

A tiny balloon is inflated to open the narrowed area. Some balloons are coated with medicine to help keep the artery open longer.

Stenting

A small mesh tube may be placed to help hold the artery open. You’ll likely take an antiplatelet medicine afterward, such as aspirin or clopidogrel (Plavix).

Your Medicines After a Stent

Take your antiplatelet medicine exactly as prescribed. Don’t stop it without talking to your specialist, even for another procedure, because stopping early can cause the stent to clot.
