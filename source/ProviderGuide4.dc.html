Referring provider guide

Joint Pain Program

Image-guided embolization reduces abnormal blood flow to inflamed synovium and tendon tissue. It is a next step for patients stuck between conservative care and surgery.

Who to Refer

✓Joint or tendon pain for 3+ months despite PT, NSAIDs and injections
✓Not ready for, not eligible for, or wanting to delay surgery
✓Knee (GAE): mild to moderate OA (KL 1–3), or pain after replacement
✓Shoulder, hip, hand (TAME): adhesive capsulitis, tendinopathy, OA
✓Heel (PFE): plantar fasciitis despite standard care

What the evidence shows

Knee GAE is the most studied. Open-label studies report meaningful pain improvement, and the SIR issued a supportive position statement in 2026.1 Small sham-controlled trials are mixed, and larger trials are under way.2 Evidence for other joints is early.3 Embolic agents are used off-label for joint pain; results vary.

Coverage

There is no GAE-specific CPT code, and many commercial payers consider joint embolization investigational. We verify benefits and obtain prior authorization before any procedure.
