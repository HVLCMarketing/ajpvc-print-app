Blood Clots & Deep Vein Problems

Problems in the deep veins can cause serious swelling and pain, and a blood clot can be dangerous. Fast testing and treatment matter.

Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

A DVT is a blood clot in a deep vein, usually in the leg. Signs include sudden swelling in one leg, pain or tenderness, warmth and redness.

Treatment is usually blood-thinning medicine. Some people need a procedure. If a clot is suspected, an ultrasound can often be done the same day.

!

Call 911

A clot can travel to the lungs. Call 911 for sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, a fast heartbeat or coughing up blood.

Deep vein blockage

A large vein in the pelvis can be squeezed or scarred, often after a past clot. This can cause swelling, heaviness and leg sores.

Venogram & deep vein stent

Contrast dye and imaging find the blockage. Angioplasty and a stent can reopen the vein and improve flow.

IVC filter

A small device placed in the large vein in the abdomen to catch clots, for people who can’t take blood thinners.

IVC filter removal

Many filters are meant to come out as soon as they’re no longer needed. Ask us when yours should come out, and keep your follow-up visits.

Lower Your Risk of Blood Clots

  • Get up and move every hour or two on long trips
  • Drink plenty of water while traveling
  • Wear compression stockings if prescribed
  • Ask about clot prevention before surgery or a hospital stay
  • Tell your doctors about any past clot or a family history of clots
