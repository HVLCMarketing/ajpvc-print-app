Genicular Artery Embolization (GAE)

An outpatient, image-guided option for knee osteoarthritis pain when conservative care has failed and surgery isn’t the right next step.

Refer when

✓Knee OA pain for 3+ months despite PT, NSAIDs and/or intra-articular injections
✓Moderate or worse pain that limits walking, sleep or work
✓Not a knee replacement candidate, or wants to delay surgery
✓Mild to moderate radiographic OA (KL 1–3); early disease tends to respond best4
✓Pain after knee replacement (investigational; case by case)

Likely not a fit

–End-stage OA better suited to replacement
–Active infection or uncorrectable coagulopathy
–Advanced CKD, severe contrast allergy or significant PAD in the leg
–Mechanical symptoms (locking) or inflammatory arthritis

What we offer

ConsultHistory, exam, imaging review and an honest candidacy discussion
ProcedureMicrocatheter through a small groin or wrist puncture; tiny particles reduce abnormal blood flow to the inflamed synovium
SettingOutpatient, local anesthetic with moderate sedation; home the same day
Follow-upVisits at 1 and 3 months; notes and outcomes sent to you

Send with the referral

•Standing knee X-rays (report or images)
•Prior treatments and injection dates
•Medication list, especially anticoagulants
•Recent eGFR; MRI if available

Evidence at a glance

SIR 2026Society of Interventional Radiology position statement supports GAE for symptomatic knee OA after failed conservative care in patients who are not replacement candidates or wish to delay surgery.1
8 → 3Median pain (0–10) at 1 year in a 40-patient open-label, single-arm U.S. trial; no control group.2
MixedSmall sham-controlled RCTs (21 to 59 patients) show mixed results; larger trials are under way.3
SafetyTemporary skin discoloration over the knee is most common; self-limited skin ulceration occurred in 7 of 40 patients in that trial.2
Coverage variesNo GAE-specific CPT code; many commercial payers consider GAE investigational. We verify benefits and obtain prior authorization. Embolic agents are used off-label for joint pain; results vary.
