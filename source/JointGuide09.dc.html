TAME for the Shoulder & Hip

Transcatheter arterial micro-embolization (TAME) uses the same approach as GAE to treat long-lasting pain from inflammation in other joints and tendons.

Shoulder

Conditions that may be treated:

  • Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis)
  • Rotator cuff tendon problems
  • Shoulder arthritis

The catheter is usually placed in an artery at the wrist. Particles go to the small vessels that feed the inflamed lining or tendon. The goal is less pain, including pain at night, and easier movement.

Hip

TAME may help people with arthritis-related hip pain who aren’t ready for, or can’t have, hip replacement.

The catheter is usually placed in an artery at the top of the thigh. Particles go to the small arteries around the lining of the hip joint.

Good to Know

Research on TAME for the shoulder and hip is at an earlier stage than for the knee, and studies so far have been smaller.

You May Be a Candidate If

  • Pain has lasted 3 months or more
  • Other treatments haven’t helped enough
  • You want to avoid or delay surgery
