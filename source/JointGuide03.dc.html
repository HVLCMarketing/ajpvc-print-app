How Pain Develops in a Joint

Knowing what happens inside a painful joint helps explain how embolization may help.

CartilageBlood vesselsInflamed liningNew nerve fibers

A Healthy Joint

In a healthy joint, cartilage covers the ends of the bones, and a thin lining makes fluid that keeps the joint moving smoothly. Small arteries bring blood to the joint and the tissue around it.

When Inflammation Takes Hold

With arthritis or a long-lasting tendon injury, the joint lining becomes inflamed. The body responds by growing extra tiny blood vessels into the area, and nerve fibers can grow alongside them. Researchers believe this mix of extra blood flow, inflammation and new nerve endings is a key source of ongoing pain.

Why This Matters

Embolization procedures target these extra blood vessels. Reducing blood flow to the inflamed tissue aims to calm the inflammation and ease pain, without surgery.
