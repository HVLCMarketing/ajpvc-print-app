Arthritis Is a Whole-Joint Disease

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • OA involves cartilage, subchondral bone, synovium, menisci, ligaments and surrounding tissues, not cartilage alone.
  • Pain can come from inflamed synovium, subchondral bone, the joint capsule, periarticular tissues and sensitized nerves.
  • Similar X-rays can hide different biology: some patients have more synovitis, others more structural or bone changes.

Treatment options

  • Education, exercise and physical therapy as the foundation
  • Weight management, bracing or a cane when appropriate
  • Topical or oral NSAIDs, weighing each patient’s risks
  • Injections or GAE if symptoms persist despite good care

Ask your patient

  • How long does morning stiffness last?
  • Are other joints swollen or painful?
  • How is the pain affecting your sleep and activity?

Refer when

  • Persistent swelling, prolonged morning stiffness, or symmetric small-joint involvement: consider inflammatory arthritis and rheumatology.
  • Hot, swollen joint with systemic symptoms: urgent evaluation for septic arthritis.
