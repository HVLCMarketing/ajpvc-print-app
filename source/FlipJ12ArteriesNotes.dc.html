The Genicular Arteries

Patient sees the facing page

Explain it simply

  • Knee blood supply includes branches associated with the popliteal artery and the descending genicular artery, including the superior and inferior medial and lateral genicular arteries.
  • These vessels connect through anastomoses and their branching varies, so one branch may supply more than one region, including skin.
  • During GAE, angiography maps this network so the specialist can treat selected abnormal branches while preserving the knee’s main blood supply.

Treatment options

  • Angiographic mapping before any treatment
  • Selective microcatheter placement
  • Protective steps such as cold packs on the skin

Ask your patient

  • Where exactly is your knee pain: inner, outer or front?
  • Have you had knee surgery or a knee replacement?
  • Do your legs cramp with walking and ease with rest?

Refer when

  • Calf, thigh or buttock pain with walking: evaluate for peripheral artery disease.
  • Weak or absent pulses, or a cold or discolored foot: vascular evaluation.
  • Non-healing leg or foot wounds: vascular evaluation.
