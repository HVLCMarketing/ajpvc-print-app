Arterial & venous guide

Evaluating Your Circulation

Your first visit is about finding the cause of your symptoms. Most circulation tests are painless and use ultrasound or blood pressure cuffs.

Your History and Exam

  • Where your legs hurt, swell or ache, and when
  • Any sores, skin changes or color changes
  • Smoking, diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol
  • Past blood clots, surgeries or procedures
  • Medicines, especially blood thinners
  • Allergies, especially to X-ray contrast dye

Your specialist will check the pulses in your legs and feet and look at your skin and veins.

Common Tests

ABI & PVR

Blood pressure cuffs compare the pressure in your ankles with your arms. A simple, painless check for PAD.

Arterial ultrasound

Sound waves show where arteries are narrowed or blocked and how fast blood is flowing.

Venous ultrasound

Checks whether vein valves leak (reflux) and looks for blood clots.

Same-day clot ultrasound*

If a clot is suspected, an ultrasound can often be done the same day.

Vein mapping

Measures your veins before some procedures, such as dialysis access or bypass.

Skin perfusion pressure

Measures blood flow in the skin to show how well a wound may heal.

Carotid ultrasound

Looks for narrowing in the neck arteries that supply the brain.

Aortic & renal ultrasound

Checks the main artery in the abdomen and the arteries to the kidneys.

*Available for most insurances. Your care team will tell you which tests you need.
