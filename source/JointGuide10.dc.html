Joint & tendon pain guide

TAME for the Hand, Wrist & Ankle

Small joints can cause big problems. Arthritis in the hand, wrist or ankle can make it hard to grip, write, open jars or walk comfortably.

Hand & Thumb

For arthritis in the finger joints and at the base of the thumb. The catheter is usually placed in an artery at the wrist.

Wrist

For long-lasting wrist pain from arthritis or an old injury. The catheter is usually placed in an artery at the wrist or elbow.

Ankle

For arthritis and ongoing ankle pain. The catheter is usually placed in an artery at the top of the thigh or near the ankle.

How It Works

Just like GAE, your specialist uses contrast dye and X-ray to find small arteries with extra blood flow around the painful joint. Tiny particles are released into those branches to reduce blood flow to the inflamed tissue.

Because the arteries in the hand and foot are very small, your specialist uses extra-fine catheters and small amounts of particles.

Lower Back Pain

Certain kinds of long-lasting lower back pain may also be evaluated for embolization. This use is still being studied. Ask your specialist whether it may be an option for you.

Good to Know

Research on TAME for these joints is at an early stage. Your specialist will talk with you about what is known, the possible benefits and risks, and other treatments.

You May Be a Candidate If

  • Pain has lasted 3 months or more
  • Medicine, splints, therapy or injections haven’t helped enough
  • Imaging shows arthritis or inflammation
  • You want to avoid or delay surgery
