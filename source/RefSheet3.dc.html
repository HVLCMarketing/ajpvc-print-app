Arterial disease

Peripheral Artery Disease & Limb Salvage

Find PAD early, treat risk factors hard, and refer chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI) urgently.

Refer when

✓Claudication that limits walking or work
✓ABI 0.90 or lower, or noncompressible (above 1.40)
✓Urgent: rest pain, a nonhealing foot or leg wound, or gangrene (CLTI)
✓Diabetic foot wound with weak or absent pulses
✓Known PAD with returning or worsening symptoms
✓Consider an ABI at age 65+; 50 to 64 with risk factors; under 50 with diabetes plus another risk factor; or known atherosclerosis elsewhere1

What we offer

TestingABI and exercise ABI, skin perfusion pressure, arterial duplex
Medical careRisk-factor optimization and exercise therapy guidance
EndovascularAngiography, balloon angioplasty, stenting and atherectomy
Wound healingCoordinated wound care and hyperbaric oxygen therapy when indicated
SurgeryWe coordinate with vascular surgery when bypass is the better option

Send with the referral

•ABI or toe pressures, if done
•Wound photos and how long it has been open
•Diabetes, kidney and smoking history
•Medication list (antiplatelet, statin, anticoagulant)

Evidence at a glance

≤0.90ABI diagnostic of PAD; 0.91 to 0.99 is borderline. Use a toe-brachial index when the ABI is above 1.40.1
25%+of patients with CLTI have a normal or borderline ABI, so a normal ABI does not rule it out.1
Class 1Structured exercise for claudication (3 times a week, 12 weeks), antiplatelet therapy and a high-intensity statin.1
Class 1Revascularization, when feasible, for CLTI to minimize tissue loss, heal wounds and preserve a functional limb.1
Time mattersCall the coordinator for rest pain or a nonhealing wound. Send acute limb ischemia (sudden cold, pale, painful limb) or an infected foot with fever or wet gangrene to the ED.

1. Gornik HL et al. 2024 ACC/AHA/AACVPR/APMA/ABC/SCAI/SVM/SVN/SVS/SIR/VESS guideline for the management of lower extremity peripheral artery disease. J Am Coll Cardiol 2024.
