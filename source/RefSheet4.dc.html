Venous disease

Venous Insufficiency & Varicose Veins

Symptomatic reflux can be treated in the office, and earlier ablation helps venous ulcers heal.

Refer when

✓Symptomatic varicose veins: aching, heaviness, cramping, itching (CEAP C2)
✓Leg swelling of venous origin (C3)
✓Skin changes: pigmentation, eczema, lipodermatosclerosis (C4)
✓Refer early: healed or active venous ulcer (C5 to C6)
✓Superficial phlebitis (duplex to exclude DVT), or a varix that has bled (after control)

Evidence at a glance

>0.5 sReflux longer than 0.5 s in superficial veins (1 s in femoral and popliteal veins) on standing duplex is pathologic.1
PreferredGuidelines favor intervention over prolonged compression for symptomatic varicose veins with axial reflux, and endovenous ablation over stripping.1
FasterEarly ablation plus compression healed venous leg ulcers faster than deferred ablation in the EVRA trial (450 patients).2
0.11 vs 0.16Ulcer recurrences per person-year, early vs deferred ablation; time to first recurrence did not differ.3
Insurance tipMany plans require documented symptoms and a compression trial before ablation. Noting the CEAP class and compression dates in your referral speeds approval.
